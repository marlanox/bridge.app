Mansionly vs Keyward: what the sounds say

Brand researchers have measured how single sounds in a name change what people feel about a brand. Below: the four findings that matter for us, how each name scores, and new names that keep the scale of Mansionly with the hard, confident sound of Keyward.

Hard consonants feel strong and reliable

Stops like K, T, D, P, B, G read as firm, sharp and decisive. Soft sounds like M, N, L, S, SH read as gentle, smooth and feminine. This is the famous “kiki / bouba” effect.

Klink 2000; Ramachandran & Hubbard 2001

Back vowels feel big and premium

“A”, “O”, “AW” (as in hall, mansion) make a brand feel larger, heavier and more luxurious. “EE”, “I” feel small, light and fast.

Yorkston & Menon 2004; Lowrey & Shrum 2007

A hard first letter is remembered better

Names that start with a stop consonant (K, C, B, D, P, T) are recalled more easily, and many top brands start that way.

Vanden Bergh et al. 1984, 1987

Easy to say = easy to trust

Names that are easy to pronounce are judged more positively and more trustworthy. Two syllables is the sweet spot for recall.

Alter & Oppenheimer 2006

Recommendation: Keyhall

It keeps what you like in Keyward (the hard K, the key, the strong logo) and adds what you like in Mansionly (a big, estate-sized sound that can carry home sales later). Two syllables, easy to say in English and Spanish, and it reads as a brand rather than a job description.
