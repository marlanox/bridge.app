The Concordance

Lay your chains side by side.

Any two passports produce a key that belongs to neither. Three produce another. The cell it opens was written for that combination only.

Two or more codes. Order does not matter.

Why people do this

Couples do it on the night they exchange chains. Brothers do it at a funeral. Strangers at a closed supper do it to find out whether they were meant to meet. The key is calculated from the codes themselves, so the same chains always produce the same key, and no other combination ever will.

Concordance pairs can be commissioned together: two chains bound on the same day, from the same length of silver, with passport codes that belong to each other.
