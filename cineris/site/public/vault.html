How a cell is sealed

When a chain is commissioned as a gift, the giver receives a private link to its cell. Inside they can place text, photographs, a voice note or a film. They choose the date it opens: tomorrow, in ninety days, on a wedding day, on an eighteenth birthday, or never, until a second chain is presented beside it.

The wearer only learns that the cell exists. When they present the code, the Vault answers with the number of days left. Not the content. Not the sender’s name, unless the sender chose to sign.

What people seal

  • A proposal, opened on the anniversary of the first date.
  • A father’s letter to a child, opened at eighteen.
  • A confession, opened after the giver is gone.
  • A note to oneself, opened in one year.
  • An accusation. We do not judge what goes in.

Cells are stored encrypted. The house cannot open a cell early, including for the wearer, the giver or us. Read the Vault terms.
