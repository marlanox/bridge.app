The Break: How to Recognise an Ordo Cineris Chain

Great houses are recognised without a logo. This is how ours is.

The jewelry that lasts is the jewelry you can identify from across a room with no logo in sight: a screw head on a bangle, an anchor link, a woven panther. We wanted one idea that strong, and we wanted it to come from how a chain is built, not from a symbol stamped on it.

The grammar

  1. Silence. Fine 2.4 mm cable.
  2. The Break. The Ash Link: a matte black oval, a silver rim, a single rivet of 18k gold. Exactly one in every chain.
  3. Weight. Without transition, hand-forged 11 mm ovals. Eight to twelve centimetres, worn on the left, over the heart.
  4. Silence again, and then one object, two at most: an old-cut diamond, a ruby, a cameo, a baroque pearl, a capsule of hair.

What we will never make

No crosses. No skulls. No wings, daggers, serpents or fleurs-de-lis. These are borrowed symbols, and borrowed symbols make jewelry look like a costume. Our objects are either rare or personal, and the only symbol on the chain is the Ash Link itself.

Why the weight sits on the left

Symmetry is decoration. Asymmetry is a decision. A chain whose mass falls to one side looks like it was made for one body and one way of standing, which it was.

See it in the atelier, where the Ash Link is already in place.
