Buying a heavy silver chain

Most of the price difference is time, not metal.

Solid or hollow

Hollow links look massive and weigh little. They dent, and cannot be repaired well. Ask for the weight in grams: a solid 55 cm chain with 4 mm wire weighs around 60–90 g; mixed chains with Byzantine sections weigh more.

Soldered links

Every link of a good chain is soldered closed. Unsoldered links can be pulled open by a snag.

The clasp

On a heavy chain, a small lobster clasp is the first thing to fail. Look for a box clasp or a long-bar toggle in proportion to the chain.

Where the money goes

The silver in a heavy chain is worth a fraction of its price. The rest is hours: forging, granulating, weaving, setting, oxidizing and burnishing by hand. A machine chain of the same weight costs a few hundred dollars. A hand-built chain with a cameo, a ruby and an 18k ring takes days of a jeweler’s work, and is priced accordingly.
