How a chain is bound

From wire to a sealed code, step by step.

  1. Measuring. Neck, sweater, preferred fall. The chain is planned link by link on paper.
  2. Links. Cable links are formed from sterling wire, closed and soldered. Balinese sections are stamped and granulated. Byzantine sections are woven ring by ring.
  3. Artifacts. The cameo is set in its bezel, the ruby closed in silver, the 18k ring forged to match.
  4. Assembly. Sections are joined in your order, with jump rings either side of every artifact so the chain moves freely.
  5. Darkening. The chain is oxidized to near black.
  6. Burnishing. Every high point is rubbed back by hand until it glows.
  7. Sealing. The hair link is filled and sealed, often with you present.
  8. Passport. The plate is engraved with the chain’s code and the sealed message cell is opened for the giver.

Most chains take nine days. Chains with several artifacts, or long Byzantine sections, take up to twenty-one.
