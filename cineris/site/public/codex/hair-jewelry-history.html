History

Hair jewelry

Before photographs, hair was the portrait.

Hair jewelry is older than the Victorians. In the late seventeenth century, Stuart crystals, small faceted rock-crystal slides with hair and gold wire beneath, commemorated the executed King Charles I and private loves alike. Georgian rings set hair under glass with inscriptions. By the 1850s and 60s hairwork had become a domestic craft: women’s magazines printed patterns, and manuals taught readers to braid hair into chains, flowers and bracelets.

Love, not only death

Hair was exchanged between lovers, between friends, between parents and children leaving home. Much hair jewelry was made for the living. It said: part of me goes with you.

How hair keeps

Clean, dry hair sealed away from air and light lasts for centuries. Two-hundred-year-old lockets still hold hair that looks cut yesterday. We seal it in borosilicate glass between silver caps, which is about as stable a container as exists.

How to give us hair

A lock about the thickness of a pencil and five centimetres long is plenty. Tie it with thread at one end before cutting. Keep some back if it is irreplaceable. See the hair link.
