925 sterling & hallmarks

Three digits that tell you what you are holding.

925 means the metal is 925 parts per thousand pure silver, or 92.5%. The other 7.5% is almost always copper. Pure silver is too soft to hold a clasp or keep a link closed; copper gives it strength while keeping silver’s colour.

What US law requires

In the United States the National Gold and Silver Stamping Act governs quality marks. An item may be marked “sterling” or “925” only if it is at least 92.5% silver, and a maker who stamps a quality mark must also stamp a registered trademark, so that someone is accountable for the claim. That is why every KEPT chain carries both the 925 stamp and the house mark.

Hallmarks elsewhere

In the United Kingdom, hallmarks are applied by independent assay offices, and sterling is shown with the lion passant. Many European countries have their own symbols. The US has no mandatory assay office; the maker’s mark is the guarantee.

Our tag

Besides stamps on the clasp, each chain carries a small oval hallmark tag near the back of the neck engraved 925. It is the one place on the chain that states, plainly, what it is.
