Tear bottles

A beautiful story that is mostly untrue, and a way to make it true.

A lachrymatory is a small bottle said to hold tears. The legend holds that Roman mourners wept into slender glass vials and buried them with the dead, and that Victorian widows carried tear bottles whose stoppers let the tears evaporate slowly; when the bottle was dry, mourning was over.

The truth

Archaeologists now believe the Roman bottles, properly called unguentaria, held perfumes and oils, not tears. The Victorian bottles were real enough, but they were a romantic revival of a story about the Romans that had never been true.

Keeping a tear, honestly

A tear is mostly water and will not survive in a sealed bottle without clouding or evaporating. So we do something simpler. We take the tear on a short thread of silk at the bench, let it dry, and seal the silk inside glass. What remains is the salt and the moment, which is all a tear ever was.
