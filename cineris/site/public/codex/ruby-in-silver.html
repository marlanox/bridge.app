Ruby Set in Silver: Why a Red Stone Looks Deeper in Dark Metal

Corundum, chromium and contrast.

Ruby is red corundum, coloured by traces of chromium, and at 9 on the Mohs scale it is the hardest gem after diamond. That hardness is why it suits a chain that knocks against buttons and tables for decades.

Why silver

Gold warms a ruby toward orange. Dark oxidized silver does the opposite: it cools and deepens the red, so a modest stone reads like a much darker, more expensive one. It also makes the ruby the only warm colour in the composition, besides a single gold ring.

What to ask about treatment

  • Heat treatment is common and stable. Most rubies on the market are heated.
  • Lead-glass filling fills fractures with glass. It makes poor stones look better and is fragile. We do not use filled stones.
  • We tell you in writing whether your stone is unheated or heat-only.

The closed bezel

We set rubies in a closed bezel, a wall of silver folded over the stone’s edge. It protects the girdle and keeps the stone flush with the chain, so nothing catches on knitwear.
