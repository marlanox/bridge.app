Memento Mori Jewelry: Remember That You Will Die

Jewelry that reminds you to live by reminding you that you won’t forever.

Memento mori, Latin for “remember that you must die,” was a familiar idea in European art from the late Middle Ages. In jewelry it appears clearly in the sixteenth and seventeenth centuries: gold rings with enamelled skulls, coffins, skeletons and inscriptions, worn by people who were very much alive.

Not gloom

The point was urgency. A skull on your finger said: do not waste the day, settle your quarrels, say what you mean. Over time memento mori jewelry merged with mourning jewelry and became more personal, naming a specific person rather than death in general.

The Order of Ash

Our house takes its name from ash, and our Burnings are a kind of memento mori: something irreplaceable is destroyed so that its absence can be carried. It is meant as a spur, not a lament.
