Byzantine chain

The densest chain there is, assembled one ring at a time.

A Byzantine chain, also called a king’s chain or birdcage, is woven from round jump rings folded back through one another in pairs. The result is a dense, rope-like column with a three-dimensional pattern that looks the same from every side.

Why it is heavy

A centimetre of Byzantine weave uses several times as much silver as a centimetre of plain cable. That weight is useful. In a mixed chain, a short Byzantine section acts like a keel: it pulls the chain straight, so it falls cleanly on knitwear instead of twisting.

Made by hand

Each ring is cut, opened, threaded and closed by hand with two pliers. A few centimetres take an hour. Machine Byzantine exists, but its rings are thin and its pattern too perfect.

In our atelier it costs more per centimetre than any other link for exactly this reason.
