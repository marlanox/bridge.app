Oxidized silver

Polished silver looks like a surgical instrument. Oxidized silver looks like it has been held.

Oxidized silver is sterling silver that has been deliberately darkened. The name is a little misleading: the dark layer is not an oxide but silver sulfide, the same compound that forms naturally as tarnish. Jewelers make it on purpose, usually with a warm solution of liver of sulfur (potassium sulfide), and then control it.

Why darken silver at all

Dark recesses and bright high points give a piece depth. Every texture, granule and engraved line becomes legible. Old church silver, antique rings and the best biker silver of the 1970s and 80s all look this way, partly through age and partly by design.

How we do it

Each finished section is darkened to near black, then burnished back by hand with steel and fine abrasives so that the surfaces that touch skin and cloth catch light. The result is two-tone silver: deep shadow, soft glow.

Does it wear off?

On the high points, slowly, and that is intended. The chain becomes brighter where you touch it most. The recesses stay dark for decades. If you ever want the original contrast back, we re-oxidize at the studio.

What to avoid

Silver dips and polishing pastes strip the finish. Wipe with a dry cloth only. See care & repair.
