History

Cameos

A portrait cut from the layers of a shell.

A cameo is a carving in relief, cut so that a pale layer stands out against a darker one. The earliest great cameos were cut in hardstone such as sardonyx in the Hellenistic and Roman worlds, for kings and emperors. In the Renaissance they became collectors’ objects; in the nineteenth century, souvenirs and keepsakes.

Shell cameos

Most cameos made since the 1800s are carved from seashell, above all helmet shells, whose layers run from white to warm brown or deep orange. The town of Torre del Greco, near Naples, has been the centre of the craft for two centuries. A carver works with gravers under magnification, and a good small cameo takes days.

Why so small

A large cameo on a man’s chain looks like costume. A cameo of 14 mm, about the size of a fingernail, does the opposite: people have to come close to see that it is a face. That proximity is the effect we want.
